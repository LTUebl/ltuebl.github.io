Pedagogical Framework

Teaching Philosophy & Curricular Pillars

Empowering biomedical engineering students through authentic hands-on inquiry, mathematical simulation, entrepreneurial curiosity, and collaborative real-world design challenges.

Pillar 1

Simulation Active Learning (SAL)

Bridging abstract mathematical physics, continuum mechanics, and cellular biology into real-time browser simulations. Students interactively manipulate spring-dashpot constants, chain conformations, and assembly indices to build intuitive physical intuition before entering the laboratory.

Integrated Courses:
BME 3303 Biomechanics · BME 4313 Tissue Mechanics · BME 5313 Cellular Mechanobiology
Pillar 2

Entrepreneurial Minded Learning (EML)

Infusing the KEEN 3Cs framework (Curiosity, Connections, Creating Value) across technical coursework. Through the "Quantified Self" (QS4EML) paradigm, students connect sensor data to customer discovery, healthcare economics, and societal impact.

Integrated Courses:
BME 1002 Intro to BME · BME 3113 Wearable Tech · BME 3002 BME Best Practices · KEEN Modules
Pillar 3

Active Collaborative & Project-Based Learning

Studio-based design and multi-semester capstones where multidisciplinary teams tackle authentic industry problems. Students iterate through breadboarding, 3D printing, firmware debugging, and client validation with partners like Lear Corporation and Henry Ford Health.

Integrated Courses:
BME 4013/4022 Capstone · EGE 1001 Eng Design · BME 3113 Studio Model
Pillar 4

Innovation & Emerging Tech

Pioneering modern engineering methodologies: from open-source biomedical hardware and Web Serial sensor dashboards to integrating Explainable AI for educator workflows (KNC 2026) and Generative AI SCAMPERing ideation techniques.

Leading Initiatives:
ASEE 2025 "Fifth Element of BME is Innovation" · KNC 2026 AI Workshop · Wearables Center

Core Curricular Course Portfolio Developed & Taught

Lawrence Technological University · Department of Biomedical Engineering

Dr. Meyer has designed and taught over 11 distinct biomedical engineering courses, creating hands-on laboratory modules, clinical simulation experiences, and entrepreneurial design challenges.

BME 3113

Wearable Technology Design

Quintessential design studio course covering sensor physics, embedded microcontroller firmware, flexible circuits, power management, BLE telemetry, and medical device ergonomics.

BME 3303 & 3301

Intro to Biomechanics & Lab

Statics, kinematics, and kinetics of human musculoskeletal systems. Integrated with mastery-based vector learning drills and open-source sensor kinematic projects.

BME 5303

Engineering in Orthopedics

Active surgical simulation learning modules where students interact with orthopedic surgical tools, synthetic bones, implant fixation mechanics, and clinical device testing.

BME 4313

Tissue Mechanics

Structure-function relationships of biological tissues (articular cartilage, ligament, bone, intervertebral disc). Integrates discrete viscoelasticity and biphasic models.

BME 6303 / 5313

Cellular Mechanobiology

Mechanotransduction, focal adhesions, cytoskeletal prestress, and polymer statistical mechanics. Supported by interactive assembly theory and random walk simulations.

BME 4013 & 4022

Biomedical Projects 1 & 2

Two-semester senior capstone design sequence solving real clinical and industrial challenges (e.g. Lear automotive seating comfort, concussions, healthcare safety).

State-of-the-Art Facilities

Experimental Biomechanics Laboratory & WTIC

Combining human movement biomechanics, wireless sensor telemetry, computational finite element models, and surgical simulation at Lawrence Tech's Taubman Complex.

Experimental Biomechanics Laboratory (EBL)

Taubman Complex J134 · Lawrence Technological University

Directing advanced biomechanical investigations in human performance, sports injury mechanisms (ACL tears, ankle sprains), and orthopedic implant stability. Integrates in vivo human subject testing with cadaveric and computational finite element models.

  • 10-camera Vicon optical motion capture system (Bonita & Basler high-speed video)
  • Dual Kistler piezoelectric ground reaction force platforms
  • 16-channel Delsys Trigno wireless surface EMG and triaxial accelerometer system
  • Dynamic drop tower, materials testing machines, and FEBio computational biomechanics suite

Wearable Technology Innovation Center (WTIC)

Multidisciplinary Research · Founded 2019

Spearheading open innovation and interdisciplinary collaborations across sensing, perception, actuation, and control to engineer next-generation robotic exoskeletons, smart compression therapy, and automotive ergonomic comfort sensors.

  • Multi-year research collaboration with Lear Corporation on automotive comfort & seating vibration
  • Clinical sensor collaborations with Henry Ford Health and hospital partners
  • Quantified-Self wearable prototypes (smart rings, instrumented knee sleeves, swing sensors)
  • Edge AI and markerless computer vision pipelines (OpenCap, MediaPipe, Jetson)

Academic Appointments & Leadership

Dr. Eric G. Meyer received his Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering (Biomechanics) from Michigan State University, investigating lower extremity sports injuries under University Distinguished Professor Roger C. Haut. He completed his post-doctoral research fellowship in cartilage tissue engineering and mechanobiology at the Centre for Bioengineering at Trinity College Dublin, Ireland under Prof. Daniel J. Kelly.

At Lawrence Technological University, Dr. Meyer serves as Associate Professor in Biomedical Engineering, previously directing the Master of Science in BME program (2014-2019) and establishing the Experimental Biomechanics Laboratory and Wearable Technology Innovation Center. In 2023, he delivered the Presidential Colloquium address for LTU Research Day.
