Pedagogical Framework

Teaching Philosophy & Curricular Pillars

Empowering biomedical engineering students through authentic hands-on inquiry, mathematical simulation, entrepreneurial curiosity, and collaborative real-world design challenges.

Pillar 1

Simulation Active Learning (SAL)

Bridging abstract mathematical physics, continuum mechanics, and cellular biology into real-time browser simulations. Students interactively manipulate spring-dashpot constants, chain conformations, and assembly indices to build intuitive physical intuition before entering the laboratory.

Integrated Courses:
BME 3303 Biomechanics · BME 4313 Tissue Mechanics · BME 5313 Cellular Mechanobiology
Pillar 2

Entrepreneurial Minded Learning (EML)

Infusing the KEEN 3Cs framework (Curiosity, Connections, Creating Value) across technical coursework. Through the "Quantified Self" (QS4EML) paradigm, students connect sensor data to customer discovery, healthcare economics, and societal impact.

Integrated Courses:
BME 1002 Intro to BME · BME 3113 Wearable Tech · BME 3002 BME Best Practices · KEEN Modules
Pillar 3

Active Collaborative & Project-Based Learning

Studio-based design and multi-semester capstones where multidisciplinary teams tackle authentic industry problems. Students iterate through breadboarding, 3D printing, firmware debugging, and client validation with partners like Lear Corporation and Henry Ford Health.

Integrated Courses:
BME 4013/4022 Capstone · EGE 1001 Eng Design · BME 3113 Studio Model
Pillar 4

Innovation & Emerging Tech

Pioneering modern engineering methodologies: from open-source biomedical hardware and Web Serial sensor dashboards to integrating Explainable AI for educator workflows (KNC 2026) and Generative AI SCAMPERing ideation techniques.

Leading Initiatives:
ASEE 2025 "Fifth Element of BME is Innovation" · KNC 2026 AI Workshop · Wearables Center

The "Learn by Doing" Learning Pyramid

National Training Laboratories & Effectual Reasoning

Traditional didactic lectures yield an average 24-hour retention rate of only 5%, whereas Practice by Doing (75%) and Teaching Others (90%) establish profound conceptual mastery. By placing actual surgical power tools and orthopaedic implants into students' hands, abstract biomechanical equations translate directly into tactile comprehension of stiffness, torsional shear, and bone-implant fixation stability.

Effectual Reasoning: Students leverage existing laboratory means (M1–M5) to explore divergent clinical opportunities (E1–E5), thinking like clinician-entrepreneurs rather than passive textbook consumers.

Problem-Based & Simulation Cycle

Kolb's Experiential Learning & Clinical Translation

The course bridges two interlocking pedagogical loops: Problem-Based Learning (PBL) (Problem Analysis → Learn Concepts → Develop Solutions → Evaluate) and Simulation-Based Learning (SBL):

  • DO (Concrete Experience): Perform simulated surgical osteotomies, reaming, and fixation.
  • OBSERVE (Reflective Observation): Debrief clinical ergonomics, bone splintering, and fixation pull-out.
  • THINK (Abstract Conceptualization): Model contact mechanics, stress shielding, and osseointegration.
  • PLAN (Active Experimentation): Rapidly prototype novel implant geometries and bench-test stability.

Core Surgical Simulation Activities Showcase (A–G)

ACTIVITY A Week 2

Build Lower Extremity Musculoskeletal Model

Students construct a 1:1 biomechanical lower limb from synthetic skeletal components. Custom-shaped foam muscle bellies (Quadriceps, Hamstrings, IT Band, TFL, Hip Abductors, and Gastrocnemius) are mapped to anatomical insertion coordinates to understand lines of action and joint stabilization.

Anatomic lines of action · Muscle moment arms
ACTIVITY B Week 3

Arthroscopic ACL Reconstruction

Simulating bone-tendon-bone and hamstring graft reconstruction. Students use tibial aiming guides, cannulated drill bits, and beath pins to drill tibial and femoral tunnels, pass tendon grafts, and achieve aperture fixation with bioabsorbable interference screws.

Tunnel isometry · Interference screw torque
ACTIVITY C Week 4

Total Hip Arthroplasty (THA)

Students perform femoral neck osteotomy, acetabular hemispherical reaming, and press-fit acetabular shell placement. They incrementally broach the femoral intramedullary canal, seat modular titanium stems, and evaluate range-of-motion to prevent dislocation.

Press-fit mechanics · Impingement analysis
ACTIVITY D Week 5

Open Reduction Internal Fixation (ORIF)

Students reduce simulated transverse and oblique diaphyseal bone fractures using reduction forceps. They contour dynamic compression plates (DCP), drill pilot holes with eccentric drill guides, gauge depths, and drive bicortical titanium screws to create interfragmentary compression.

Compression plating · Bicortical purchase
ACTIVITY E Week 6

Posterior Lumbar Spine Stabilization

Working on multi-segment synthetic lumbar spines, students identify pedicle entry anatomy, probe pedicle pathways with gear-shift probes, verify bony boundaries, insert polyaxial pedicle screws, contour connecting cobalt-chrome spinal rods, and torque set screws.

Pedicle trajectory · Spinal biomechanics
ACTIVITY F Week 8

Dental Implants & Osseodensification

Sponsored in collaboration with Versah LLC. Students execute clinical surgical osteotomy protocols under continuous saline irrigation. They compare conventional bone-excavating drills with patented Densah® osseodensification burs, recording insertion torque and primary stability.

Insertion torque (N·cm) · Trabecular compaction
ACTIVITY G Week 9

Patient-Specific 3D Cranioplasty Skull Plate

Students import DICOM clinical CT patient scans into Materialise Mimics and 3-matic. They threshold bone density, reconstruct complex cranial defects, digitally model a patient-specific cranial repair prosthesis, 3D print the prosthesis, and verify surgical margin congruence.

DICOM CT segmentation · Additive manufacturing
VIRTUAL REALITY Week 1

Immersive Virtual Surgery & Haptic Force Feedback

Using 3D Systems Touch haptic robotic arms integrated with HTC Vive VR headsets, students experience realistic kinematic resistance when sawing bone, reaming the acetabular cup, or encountering cortical bone break-through, bridging physical and computational modeling.

Haptic force feedback · Virtual anatomy
INNOVATION Week 10

PBL: Clinician-Engineer Opportunity Creation

Authentic prompt: "A surgeon has contacted you through the LTU Innovation Center / Centrepolis Accelerator because of your expertise in Biomedical Engineering: Build and Test a new type of Orthopedic Implant." Student teams propose concepts, prototype devices, test biomechanics, and draft FDA 510(k) strategies.

Key Design Frameworks Analyzed by Students:

1. Human-Centered Design (IDEO) Empathize → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test. Prioritizing end-user comfort, psychological adoption, and non-stigmatizing form factors.
2. Double Diamond Design Model Divergent & convergent phases: Discover (explore problem) → Define (focus) → Develop (ideate solutions) → Deliver (prototype & ship).
3. FDA Medical Device Design Controls 21 CFR 820.30: User Needs ↔ Design Inputs ↔ Design Outputs ↔ Medical Device. Rigorous Design Verification & Validation (V&V).
4. Lean Hardware Prototyping Build – Measure – Learn sprints. Rapidly building breadboards, testing battery life, gathering telemetry, and iterating microcontroller firmware.

Step-by-Step Industrial Disassembly

Step-by-Step Reverse Engineering Protocol:

1
Functional Pre-Check
Check that your device is functional and operating as expected prior to disassembling. Record starting battery voltage or display response.
2
Sequential Disassembly & Joint Documentation
Document the steps to disassemble and record how each component was connected together (e.g., snap fits, Phillips screws, sonic welds, elastomer zebra strips, wire-bonded IC blobs).
3
Comprehensive Bill of Materials (BOM)
Produce a complete list of all components and count the number of similar components. Use actual engineering terminology when possible (e.g., cantilevered spring pendulum, conductive rubber contact, FR4 PCB, coin-cell terminal).
4
Component Function, Cost Estimation & Manufacturing Analysis
Describe each component's engineering function, estimate component costs at 10,000-unit scale, and identify potential commercial suppliers or the likely manufacturing processes (injection molding, stamping, wave soldering).
5
System Process & Interaction Diagram
Produce a system process diagram illustrating how the pedometer and selected electromechanical components work together: from physical acceleration to pendulum displacement, electrical contact closure, debouncing logic, digital counting register, and LCD multiplexing.
Submitted Worksheet Requirements Checklist:
  • Pictures of your disassembly methods: High-resolution photographs showing casing separation, internal chassis, fasteners, and PCB subassemblies.
  • Components list (BOM): Itemized table specifying component name, count, material, function, estimated unit price, and fabrication method.
  • Process diagram: Block flowchart demonstrating how components work together mechanically and electronically to measure and display steps.
